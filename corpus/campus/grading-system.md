# Grading System

The 10-point scale, letter grades, grade points, and the formulas used to compute SGPA and CGPA.

## Grading scale

Grades and their grade points are: AA = 10, AB = 9, BB = 8, BC = 7, CC = 6, CD = 5, DD = 4, F = 0.
A grade of DD is the minimum passing grade; any course graded F must be repeated through the
supplementary examination cycle under the backlog and attempts policy.

## Component weights

The course grade is composed of the end-term examination (40 percent), two sessional tests
(20 percent each), and assignments plus laboratory work (20 percent combined). Sessional test dates
are announced in the first week of the term and are not rescheduled for individual students.

## SGPA computation

The semester grade point average (SGPA) is the credit-weighted mean of the grade points earned in
that semester: SGPA equals the sum over courses of credits times grade points, divided by the sum of
credits registered in that semester. Grades of W, which denote an approved withdrawal, are excluded
from both sums, in line with the course withdrawal policy.

## CGPA computation

The cumulative grade point average (CGPA) is the credit-weighted mean of the grade points earned
across all completed semesters. CGPA is recomputed after every result publication and is the single
number used for scholarship thresholds, branch transfer eligibility, minor and honours admission,
and placement eligibility.

## Rounding

SGPA and CGPA are reported to two decimal places, rounded to the nearest value. All threshold
comparisons, such as the 8.5 requirement for scholarship renewal, use the rounded figure and not the
unrounded internal value.

## Backlogs in CGPA

A course graded F is included in CGPA with zero grade points until it is cleared by a supplementary
pass, after which the new grade replaces the F in the cumulative computation. Grades obtained in
supplementary examinations carry no distinction marks, so a supplementary pass is reported as DD
regardless of raw marks if the raw total is below the DD boundary in that revaluation.
